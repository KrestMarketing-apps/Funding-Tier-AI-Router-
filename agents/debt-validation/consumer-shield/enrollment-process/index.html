What this SOP covers

Consumer Shield (branded Shield Services in-market) is Funding Tier's debt validation program — built on the legal right to make a debt collector prove a debt is valid, accurate, and legally theirs to collect, rather than negotiating a settlement on it.

Who qualifies

$4,000+Min. total enrolled debt
$100+Min. debt per account

Blocked states: NC, PA, CO, WA, CT, NJ, OR, TX. Always re-confirm eligibility in the Router before enrolling — these thresholds and the blocked-state list live there as the source of truth.

The 4-prong approach

This is the core pitch every agent should be able to explain in one breath.

1 · Validate & resolve

Dispute and resolve enrolled debt toward a zero balance; watch collectors for violations.

2 · Lower bills

Work to bring down common monthly expenses — electric, cable/internet, cell phone.

3 · Build credit

Secured credit-builder tradeline reports on-time payments to all three bureaus.

4 · Legal protection

Membership to a national litigation law firm (NLLG), A+ BBB rating.

This is not a loan or consolidation Consumer Shield is a debt validation program, not a loan, not debt consolidation, and not debt settlement. Never describe it as any of those to a prospect — see the Compliance & Objections tab for exact language.

2. How to pull credit

Watch it once before your first live enrollment, then come back to it any time as your reference for this step.

You need two things to pull credit: the client's date of birth and Social Security number. On a pushed file these should already be filled in — confirm them before you pull.

A. Confirm DOB and SSN on the Contact Info tab

  1. Enter or confirm the DOB.
  2. Enter the SSN as nine digits only — no dashes, no spaces. When you click out of the field the system adds the dashes for you.
The SSN masks as soon as you save After Save, the first five digits show as xxx-xx- and can't be read back. Read the full SSN back to the client before you save. If you see “this social already exists,” the SSN is already on another file — stop and find that file rather than working around it.

B. Pull the report and import the creditors

Scroll to the button row at the bottom of Contact Info.

  1. Click Save to save the DOB and SSN.
  2. Click Get Credit Report. Wait for the “Report has been saved successfully” message at the top of the screen.
  3. Click Import Trades/Creditors. A good pull shows “Credit report imported successfully.”

C. Review creditors — add any missing accounts by hand

Open the Creditors tab. Each imported account appears as a line item with the creditor name, balance and account number. The Creditors tab is also where you add accounts by hand — if the pull shows a dash, or an account the client wants to enroll didn't come through on the report, click Add New and fill in:

  1. Current Creditor/Collector — the creditor's name (e.g. Chase).
  2. Current Creditor/Collector Account Number.
  3. Original Account Number — the same account number again. The account number goes in twice; the creditor name does not.
  4. Balance.
  5. Payment — what the client pays on it each month, if anything.
  6. Current Account — Yes or No, whether they're current.
  7. Click Add.

The Total Debts figure is what sets the program payment in Step 3, so make sure every account the client is enrolling is on this tab.

D. Debts that don't show on the credit report — get the statement

Not every eligible debt is on the credit report. Consumer Shield pulls Equifax only, and several kinds of debt the program accepts often never report to a bureau at all:

Personal loansSmaller banks, credit unions and online lenders that don't report to Equifax.
Tribal loansAlmost never on a credit report. Still conditional — confirm there is no wage assignment before you enroll one.
Hospital & medical billsMost never reach the report, and smaller or paid medical collections are left off it.
Why the numbers don't match When we qualify, we ask for total unsecured debt and name personal loans, medical bills, personal credit cards, store cards and accounts in collections. So when a prospect says “about $15,000,” that estimate usually includes those loans and bills. If the report comes back lower, it doesn't mean they overstated. It means some of their debt doesn't show on this report. Always compare what they told you with the Total Debts on the Creditors tab.
This is the moment to go get it By now the prospect is engaged, has given you their Social Security number and has watched you pull their credit. That's real trust, so use it. Spending an extra 10–15 minutes to find a missing statement can add thousands in eligible debt to the program, and it covers debts that would otherwise keep calling them. Don't end the call to “have them look for it later” unless you've tried the options below.
Talk track — when the report comes back short “Okay, I've got your report up. Earlier you mentioned about $15,000, and I'm seeing about $9,200 here. That's really common. Personal loans, tribal or online loans and medical bills often don't show on the credit report. Which of your loans or bills am I not seeing? … Let's get those added so they're covered too. It only takes a few minutes, and I'll walk you through it.”

Way A — the client's online account

  1. Ask: “Do you have an online account or app with [lender] that you can log into?”
  2. Have the client log in on their own device and open their most recent statement (or the account summary page showing the balance).Never ask for their username or password, and never log in for them. They do the login; you guide them.
  3. Have them download it as a PDF or take a screenshot, then email or text it to you while you stay on the line.

Way B — call the creditor together on a 3-way call

  1. Look up the creditor's customer-service number on the creditor's own website (not a search ad or a third-party directory). For a medical bill, use the billing number on the hospital's or provider's site, or the collection agency's number if it's in collections.
  2. Offer the call:“Would you like me to get [creditor] on a 3-way call with us so they can email you a copy of your statement? You'd just need to verify it's you.”
  3. Conference the creditor in. The client does the talking. They verify their identity and ask for a copy of their latest statement to be emailed to them.Don't speak for the client, don't discuss the program, the balance or a settlement with the creditor, and don't give the creditor your contact details. You're only there to help the client ask for a copy.
  4. Stay on the line until the email arrives, then have the client forward it to you.

Way C — a separate credit report that shows the debt

If the debt is on another bureau's report (TransUnion or Experian) but not on the Equifax pull, that report works as documentation. The client can get their own reports from all three bureaus free at AnnualCreditReport.com, or send one they already have (for example, from a credit-monitoring app). Have them send you the page that shows the account.

Add it and upload it

First save the statement to your computer. If the client sent a photo or screenshot, save it as a PDF, because PDF is the format the upload is built for. Then add the creditor and attach the statement to that creditor.

1 · Add the creditor

  1. On the Creditors tab, click Add New, under Total Debts on the right.
  2. Creditor Type: leave it on ATC.
  3. Current Creditor/Collector: who the client owes now, as named on the latest statement or letter. If the account is in collections, that's the collection agency.
  4. Current Creditor/Collector Account Number: the account number on that statement or letter.
  5. Original Creditor: who the debt started with, e.g. the hospital or the lender. If it has never been sold or sent to collections, it's the same as #3.
  6. Original Account Number: the original account number. If it's the same account, enter the same number again, just as in section C.
  7. Creditor Statement Address: the payment or mailing address printed on the statement.
  8. City, State, Zip of that address.
  9. Collector: the collection agency, if the account is in collections. Leave it blank if it isn't.
  10. Balance: the current balance on the statement. Never estimate it.
  11. Payment: what the client pays on it each month, if anything.
  12. Current Account: Yes or No, whether the client is current on it.
  13. Click Add. The creditor now appears on the Creditors tab, and Total Debts goes up.

2 · Attach the statement to that creditor

  1. Scroll down to the creditor you just added. Set its Creditor Status. On a medical bill, fill in Date of Service if it's on the statement, or tick Client did Not Provide.
  2. In Creditor Notes, write where the proof came from, e.g. “Statement from client's online account, 9/24/26” or “Statement emailed by creditor on 3-way call, 9/24/26.”
  3. Click Select File, at the bottom right of that creditor's section.Use the Select File for this creditor, so the statement is filed against the right account.
  1. In the Upload document window, type a tag such as statement in Tags.
  2. Click Select Files and choose the statement PDF.
  3. Wait for “Document upload processing…” to finish. Don't close the page or click away while it spins.
  4. Confirm the file now shows under Creditor Correspondence with the date and time. Click Edit File Name to give it a clear name, e.g. Statement – New Hope Medical – Sept 2026.
  5. Check that Total Debts is right. It may move the client into a higher payment tier in Step 3.
If you can't get the statement on this call Don't lose the enrollment over it. Enroll the accounts you've verified, note the missing lender and balance on the file, and set a follow-up for when the client has the statement. Ask your team lead how to add the account once it arrives. Never guess a balance or account number.

Post-enrollment · exception management

Declines, NSFs and re-enrollments

What to do when a Consumer Shield file comes back after submission. Follow these steps before you return a file to Underwriting or assign a task to the Payment Changes Department.

STOP: fixing the problem is not enough

Never move a file forward just because the underlying issue has been corrected. Every required step below (new documents, signatures, client “YES” texts, status change, department task) has to be done, in order, before the file or task moves.

Any correction means ALL documents are resent and signed, never just the corrected page. The only exception is the ATC, which is not resent. A prior signed agreement can never be reused.

Declined file

Declined: Genie incomplete

Underwriting declined the file because the AI Genie call is missing or incomplete.

  3. Update the file status to Pending Underwriting Review.

Declined: file or documentation issue

Wrong payment date, wrong payment amount, missing bank information, or any other error on the file.

  2. Resend ALL documents. A corrected file needs a complete new document package sent to the client for signature, the same way as Step 5.

Cancelled client

Re-enrollment after cancellation

Applies to clients cancelled for ANY reason, including NSF cancellation and cancellation before the first draft.

Update payment / banking→ALL new docs signed (not ATC)→Client texts “YES” to date + re-enrolling→Payment Changes task
  1. Update the payment information: the new payment amount, payment date and cancel date, as applicable.
  2. Update the banking information if needed. Confirm the client's current banking details are accurate.
  3. Send ALL new documents. The client must receive and sign a complete new package.
  4. Confirm all documents are signed. Don't go further until the full package is signed.
  5. Text the client to confirm the new payment date.
  6. Confirm the re-enrollment. The same text, or a separate one, must also confirm that the client wants to re-enroll in the program. The client must reply “YES”.
  7. Create a Payment Changes Department task. Only after every step above is done. State clearly what was corrected and the new payment date.

"How do I know this is legit?"

"Totally fair. You're protected by a membership to a national litigation law firm with an A+ BBB rating. You're never doing this alone."

"I can't afford anything right now."

"I hear you — that's exactly why we also work to lower your everyday bills like electric, internet, and your phone. The goal is to put money back in your pocket."

"I can't promise a specific result — every debt is different and outcomes vary. What I can tell you is exactly how the process works and how hard we work on your behalf."

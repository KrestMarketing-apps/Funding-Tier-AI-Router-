PHASE 1 Always Ask — Before Any Pitch
2
Universal Gates — All Must PassAlways asked
Ask these five before discussing any program. A "no" on gates A–D is a hard stop. Gate E (already enrolled) is the exception — an obstacle to overcome, not a disqualifier.
A
Is the debt unsecured?
All three programs work unsecured debt only — credit cards, personal loans, medical, collections, etc. Secured debt (auto loans, mortgages) and cross-collateralized accounts don't qualify. ↗ check Router for accepted vs. rejected debt types and creditors.
No unsecured debt → not a fit. Do not enroll.
B
Are they employed or do they have steady income?
"Are you currently employed, self-employed, retired, or receiving another steady source of income?" A program only works if there's reliable income behind the monthly deposit.
No income source → cannot sustain the program.
C
Willingness — are they currently trying to pay this debt down?
"Are you currently making payments on these debts, or trying to?" You're confirming they take the debt seriously and want to resolve it — payment willingness.
D
Ability — can they afford at least ~$250/month?
"If we could set up a single, more manageable monthly amount, would something around $250 a month be workable for you?" ELP and Level Debt both require a $250 minimum monthly — payment ability. This is the make-or-break affordability gate.
Can't do ~$250/mo → likely only Consumer Shield (lower tiers); confirm in Router.
E
Are they already enrolled in another debt-relief program?Obstacle — not a stop
"Are you currently enrolled in any other debt settlement, debt relief, or credit program right now?" If yes — this is not a disqualifier. They're already paying someone every month, which means there's willingness and ability there. The play is to win them over with a better deal — usually a lower monthly, depending on which program they qualify for.
"That actually tells me you're serious about handling this — which is great. A lot of those programs lock people into a payment that's higher than it needs to be. Let me find out what you're paying now, because depending on which of our programs fits, we may be able to do this for less per month and still get you to the finish line."

4
Why Debts Get Dismissed — The Legal Grounds
Here's the part that gives the program its teeth. The collection industry is sloppy — debts get bought, sold, and re-sold for pennies, and along the way records get lost, mangled, or never properly transferred. Federal law puts the burden of proof on them, not your client. ELP's attorneys go hunting for the breakdowns below, and any one of them can be grounds to challenge or invalidate a debt.
Phone Script — Build the Value
"Let me tell you why this works. Under federal law — the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act — a collector has to prove a debt is valid, accurate, and legally theirs to collect. The catch is, most of these debts have been bought and sold so many times that the paperwork is a mess. When ELP's attorneys demand proper validation and the collector can't produce it, that debt can be challenged and wiped out. We're not asking them nicely — we're holding them to the law."
Grounds ELP's attorneys look for
📄
Failure to validate the debt
Once disputed, the collector must produce proof you owe the exact amount they claim. Many can't — they bought the account without complete records.
FDCPA §809
🔗
Broken chain of ownership
Debt is sold from buyer to buyer. If they can't show an unbroken paper trail proving they legally own it, they may have no standing to collect.
Standing to collect
🧾
Inaccurate or unverifiable amounts
Wrong balances, padded interest, or fees that don't match the original agreement. If the number can't be verified, the reporting can be challenged.
FCRA §611
⏰
Time-barred / past statute
Old debts can fall outside the legal window to sue on them. Collectors who misrepresent a time-barred debt as currently enforceable are violating the law.
Statute of limitations
📵
Harassment & abusive contact
Repeated calls, calls at odd hours, threats, or contacting you after you're represented are violations — each one can strengthen your case and may carry penalties.
FDCPA §806–807
🚫
Continuing to contact after cease
Once you're a represented client and the collector is notified, continued direct contact is a violation — which can accelerate resolution and create leverage.
FDCPA §805
⚖️
False or misleading representations
Misstating the amount, the legal status, or who they are. The law prohibits deceptive collection tactics — and these surface more often than people think.
FDCPA §807
📊
Improper credit reporting
Reporting a disputed or unverified debt, duplicate listings, or re-aging an old account. Inaccurate reporting is challengeable and can lead to deletion.
FCRA
Phone Script — Bring It Home
"So while a regular person calling a collector gets nowhere, ELP's attorneys come at it from the legal side — demanding validation, auditing the paperwork, and documenting every violation along the way. Every misstep they find works in your favor. That's how accounts get challenged, disputed, and resolved — and it's all handled by the legal team, not you."
Keep it accurate
These are the legal grounds ELP's attorneys pursue — not a promise that any specific debt will be dismissed. Outcomes depend on the individual file and the collector's records. Never guarantee a result.

Why Debts Get Dismissed — The Legal Grounds
Here's the part that gives the program its teeth. The collection industry is sloppy — debts get bought, sold, and re-sold for pennies, and along the way records get lost, mangled, or never properly transferred. Federal law puts the burden of proof on them, not your client. ELP's attorneys go hunting for the breakdowns below, and any one of them can be grounds to challenge or invalidate a debt.
Phone Script — Build the Value
"Let me tell you why this works. Under federal law — the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act — a collector has to prove a debt is valid, accurate, and legally theirs to collect. The catch is, most of these debts have been bought and sold so many times that the paperwork is a mess. When ELP's attorneys demand proper validation and the collector can't produce it, that debt can be challenged and wiped out. We're not asking them nicely — we're holding them to the law."
Grounds ELP's attorneys look for
📄
Failure to validate the debt
Once disputed, the collector must produce proof you owe the exact amount they claim. Many can't — they bought the account without complete records.
FDCPA §809
🔗
Broken chain of ownership
Debt is sold from buyer to buyer. If they can't show an unbroken paper trail proving they legally own it, they may have no standing to collect.
Standing to collect
🧾
Inaccurate or unverifiable amounts
Wrong balances, padded interest, or fees that don't match the original agreement. If the number can't be verified, the reporting can be challenged.
FCRA §611
⏰
Time-barred / past statute
Old debts can fall outside the legal window to sue on them. Collectors who misrepresent a time-barred debt as currently enforceable are violating the law.
Statute of limitations
📵
Harassment & abusive contact
Repeated calls, calls at odd hours, threats, or contacting you after you're represented are violations — each one can strengthen your case and may carry penalties.
FDCPA §806–807
🚫
Continuing to contact after cease
Once you're a represented client and the collector is notified, continued direct contact is a violation — which can accelerate resolution and create leverage.
FDCPA §805
⚖️
False or misleading representations
Misstating the amount, the legal status, or who they are. The law prohibits deceptive collection tactics — and these surface more often than people think.
FDCPA §807
📊
Improper credit reporting
Reporting a disputed or unverified debt, duplicate listings, or re-aging an old account. Inaccurate reporting is challengeable and can lead to deletion.

Answering an inbound call

This SOP covers what happens when a live call lands in your queue at app.krestmarketing.com — from the moment you pick up through pushing the prospect's data to the correct backend and starting the soft credit pull.

Where these calls come from These are live inbound transfers, not cold outreach. The prospect responded to an RVM (ringless voicemail) or SMS about a hardship loan or debt pre-qualification offer, then got routed into the call queue. That means you already have their phone number before you say a word — don't ask for it again, confirm it.

Step 1

Capturing contact information

Enter these fields in order, directly into the contact record in app.krestmarketing.com, while you're still on the call.

Already known

Phone Number

You already have this from the call transfer. Read it back to the prospect to confirm it's correct and reachable — don't re-ask for it as if it's new information.

Client data capture

First Name & Last Name

Get the full legal name, spelled if it's unusual. This is what will appear on every downstream document.

Client data capture

Email Address

Read it back letter by letter, including the domain, before moving on.

Client data capture

Street Address

Full mailing address — street number, street name, apartment/unit if applicable.

Client data capture · system fix required

State

Always select the state from the dropdown list — never type it in freehand.

Why this matters Typing a state in as free text (e.g. typing "CA" into an open field instead of picking California from the list) breaks state-based eligibility and compliance logic downstream, since those systems match against the dropdown's stored values, not arbitrary text. Always click the field and choose the state from the list that appears.
Client data capture

Postal Code

Full 5-digit ZIP code. Confirm it matches the state selected above.

Step 2

Collecting the Social Security Number

The SSN is required to run the soft credit pull. Some prospects are uncomfortable saying it out loud on a phone call — that's expected, and there's a workaround for it.

Why we need it The SSN is what lets the backend (Level Debt, ELP, or Consumer Shield) pull the prospect's credit report and verify their identity in the Soft Credit Pull & Verification step. Without it, the file can't move past qualification — there's no way to confirm the debts the prospect described or run identity verification.
Standard path

Ask directly

Ask for the SSN and enter it into the SSN field on the contact record. Read the last four digits back to confirm before moving on.

Optional · workaround

SSN Secure Form Collector

If the prospect doesn't want to say their SSN out loud, don't push them to say it — use the SSN Secure Form Collector instead. Send the secure link to them (by text or email, whichever they confirm they can access right now, while you stay on the line) so they can enter their own SSN directly into an encrypted form rather than speaking it to you.

Step 3

Qualification questions

Ask these before pushing the file to a backend — each one exists for a specific reason, not just to fill in a field. Know why you're asking, not just what to ask.

The actual contact record fields in app.krestmarketing.com — Estimated Amount of Unsecured Debt, Total Amount Paid Each Month, Are You Currently Employed, Marital Status, Date of birth, and Social Security Number, all highlighted
Country is pre-populated The Country field defaults to United States already — you don't need to ask for it or set it manually. Leave it as-is unless the prospect specifically isn't a US resident.
Why it matters · sizes the opportunity

Estimated Amount of Unsecured Debt

This gives you a read on the size of the opportunity and which program(s) the prospect can even qualify for.

Always confirm the debt is actually unsecured Most people don't know what "unsecured" means, so don't just ask "how much unsecured debt do you have" and take the number at face value — walk them through it. Unsecured debt is things like credit cards, personal loans, medical bills, and collections. It is not a student loan, a car loan, or a solar panel loan — those are secured or otherwise excluded and will throw off eligibility if counted in the total.
Why it matters · ability & willingness to pay

Total Amount Paid Each Month Towards Debts

This isn't just a data point — it's your read on whether this prospect actually pays their bills. Someone who has a track record of making monthly payments toward their debt is a much better bet to keep paying once they're in a program. If they're already behind on everything, that's a signal worth noting, not ignoring.

Why it matters · ability to pay

Are You Currently Employed?

Employment is the most direct signal of whether the prospect can actually afford the program payment going forward. Get a clear yes/no, and if relevant, what kind of income (job, self-employed, fixed income) it is.

Why it matters · affects the application

Marital Status

Marital status affects how the application and enrollment are structured. If the prospect is married, always follow up with the next question — don't skip it.

If married: are the debts joint or individual? Ask whether the enrolled debts are in joint accounts (both spouses' names) or only in the prospect's name. This changes who's liable for the debt and needs to be captured accurately — don't assume either way.
Non-negotiable · required for every program

Date of Birth & Social Security Number

Both must be entered correctly — the soft credit pull will fail or return the wrong person's data if either is off by even one digit. Read both back to the prospect to confirm before moving on.

This applies to every program, no exceptions Level Debt, Elite Legal Practice, and Consumer Shield all require an accurate DOB and SSN to run their soft credit pull. There is no program where you can skip or approximate these fields. See the SSN Collection tab if the prospect doesn't want to say their SSN out loud.
Step 4

Pushing the file to a backend

Once contact info, SSN, and qualification questions are all captured, the file is ready to go to Level Debt, Elite Legal Practice, or Consumer Shield. This happens by applying the correct tag to the contact — the tag triggers an automation that pushes the data via webhook.

How it works

Open Select tags on the contact record, search, and apply the one tag that matches where this file is going. Applying the tag is what fires the webhook — nothing pushes until the tag is added.

Available push tags

push to elp + level – (forth + sales force) push to consumer shield push to forth crm | level push to legacy / elp / sales force
Only apply one push tag Each tag routes the file to a different destination system (or combination of systems). Applying the wrong one — or more than one — sends the prospect's data somewhere it doesn't belong. Confirm the program from the qualification questions before tagging.
This is why accuracy here matters Applying the tag pushes everything you entered — name, phone, email, address, DOB, SSN, qualification answers — directly into the backend (Forth, Salesforce, or Consumer Shield's system) via webhook. The agent enrolling this file on the backend side should not need to re-type any of it; they're instructed to check that it came through correctly rather than re-enter it from scratch. A mistake made here is a mistake that follows the file everywhere it goes next.

Step 5

Soft credit pull & identity verification

Once the file has been pushed to the backend, that system is what runs the credit pull and identity check — not app.krestmarketing.com itself.

What a soft credit pull is

A soft credit pull (also called a "soft inquiry") checks a prospect's credit report and score without affecting their credit score and without appearing to other lenders as an inquiry. It's used here purely to confirm eligibility and pull in the debt/creditor information needed to build the program — not to make a lending decision. This is different from a "hard pull," which does affect credit score and is typically only used when a lender is extending new credit.

Explaining it to the prospect

If they ask what this means for their credit: a soft pull does not lower their score, does not show up to other lenders, and is not the same as applying for a new loan or credit card. It's simply how we confirm the debt information they've already told us about.

Verifying identity

Primary · Spinwheel (via Forth)

Forth's built-in Spinwheel integration verifies identity automatically as part of running the credit pull, using the contact information already on file.

Fallback · KBA

If Spinwheel can't confirm identity automatically, fall back to Knowledge-Based Authentication (KBA) — a short set of multiple-choice questions pulled from the prospect's credit history (e.g. past addresses or loans) that only the real person should be able to answer.
